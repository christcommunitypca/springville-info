Springville Once Supplied Iron Ore to Three States

In 1880, Springville was home to one of Alabama’s largest iron ore mining operations. A single local mine produced more than 12,000 tons of ore, and Springville supplied iron furnaces in three states.

Springville Once Attracted Visitors With Giant Carp

Mayor James McLaughlin stocked Springville’s former lake with carp that grew large enough to become a local attraction. By the 1950s, families traveling through town were stopping to feed the fish by hand.

Springville Once Had Its Own College

Spring Lake College once drew students to Springville from as far away as Texas. Some boarded with local families in the early 1900s.

Springville Has St. Clair County's Only Cast-Iron Storefront

Did you know the Presley Store at 601 Main Street has the only cast-iron storefront in St. Clair County? Built in 1902 for brothers Porter and John Presley, it's a one-of-a-kind part of downtown Springville.

Summer Readers Grow More Than 3,200%

When Jamie Twente arrived in 2015, Springville Public Library's previous summer reading record was just 25. In 2026, participation reached 829—more than 3,200% above that earlier mark. A remarkable achievement for Twente and the library team.

Springville's downtown is a nationally recognized historic district

Springville's historic district joined the National Register of Historic Places on July 3, 1997, recognizing the architecture and history of its old downtown.

On January 16, 1969, a train derailed at the Springville Depot. Its cars struck propane tanks, setting off explosions and a fire.
