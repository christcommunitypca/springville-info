The night a train explosion shook Springville

Did you know? · AI-written · Published November 20, 2026

On January 16, 1969, a train derailed at the Springville Depot. Its cars struck propane tanks, setting off explosions and a fire.

Firefighters came from as far away as Birmingham, and the National Guard joined the response. Buildings were damaged, but no one was killed.

When Springville was Pinkhill

Did you know? · AI-written · Published November 19, 2026

For a brief time in the 1830s, a letter bound for what is now Springville could be addressed to Pinkhill.

The settlement’s earlier name was Big Springs. Fresh water made it a stopping place for Native Americans and, later, settlers traveling through the area. When the first post office opened in 1833, the name on the mail was Pinkhill.

Pinkhill had a short run. In December 1834 the name changed to Springville, bringing the springs back into the town’s name. Of the town’s three names, two point to the water that first drew people here.

Springville's Charlie Hare signs with Gadsden State

Published November 17, 2026

Springville High School senior Charlie Hare signed to play baseball for Gadsden State, with one more season ahead for the Tigers.

Springville High School center fielder Charlie Hare signed his letter of intent on Nov. 12 to play college baseball for the Gadsden State Cardinals, according to a Nov. 17 report in The Trussville Tribune.

Hare's junior season was cut short by injury. Before then he was batting .375 with a .460 on-base percentage. He plans to return for one more Tigers season, expected to begin in March.
