Thankful for the Town We Call Home

A Thanksgiving reflection on the town we call home.

November 25, 2026

Thanksgiving has me thinking about Springville.

When I first came here a little over ten years ago, Springville was already a wonderful place to raise a family. It was welcoming, comfortable, and full of good people. It also had room to grow.

And grow it has.

We've gained conveniences, new businesses, better facilities, and opportunities that weren't here when we arrived. Yet somehow, Springville has managed to mature without losing so much of what drew us here in the first place.

I'm thankful for that.

I'm thankful for Big Springs Park and the beauty of the water flowing through the heart of our town. There's something about watching that water make its way over the rocks that invites you to slow down and appreciate the place God has given us.

I'm thankful for Big Canoe Creek Nature Preserve. What a remarkable gift to be able to walk among the trees, listen to the creek, and wonder at God's creation without ever leaving Springville. I'm especially thankful for the people who had the vision to preserve that land and the perseverance to make it accessible for the rest of us.

I'm thankful for our downtown. The improvements over the past several years have made it a clean, safe, and inviting place to spend time. I'm thankful for the businesses that have been part of Springville for years, and for the new shops and restaurants that have joined them. The new doesn't have to replace the old. Together, they're giving us more reasons to enjoy our own town.

I'm thankful for those who have led Springville through the years.

During Mayor Butch Isley's tenure, the city was investing in recreation and working toward improvements along our downtown streets. During Mayor Dave Thomas's years, we saw continued investment in community gathering places, including improvements to the farmers market and the opening of the nature preserve. I'm thankful for council members like Katrina Hennings, who served through many years of growth and helped guide improvements to our parks, schools, and infrastructure.

I'm thankful for Mayor Austin Phillips and our current City Council as they continue building on that legacy. The work toward a new commerce park brings exciting possibilities for Springville's future, and the city's comprehensive planning survey gives all of us an opportunity to help shape the next chapter. I'm grateful for leaders who are looking ahead and inviting our neighbors to be part of that conversation.

And behind those names are countless others—city employees, board members, volunteers, business owners, and citizens—who have invested their time and energy in making Springville what it is today.

I'm thankful for our schools, for the new middle school building that represents an investment in our growing community, and especially for the teachers and administrators who work year after year to provide a good place for our children to learn. Buildings are important, but it's the people inside them who make the difference.

I'm thankful for our athletic programs. My children have benefited from them for years, and I couldn't begin to count the people who have poured themselves into those opportunities. Coaches, parents, volunteers, and everyone who has given up an evening or a Saturday to make something special possible for our kids. I'm thankful for the investments in our fields and sports facilities, too. These are places where children grow, friendships form, and families make memories.

My sons have gained so much through sports, and my daughters found something equally special at 5678 Dance & More. They loved dancing, and the studio gave them a place to develop their gifts, build friendships, and use the talents God had given them for his glory. I'm thankful for the teachers and families who invested in them along the way. Opportunities like these have meant so much to our family.

And I'm especially thankful for the pulpits in our town that proclaim the excellencies of Jesus Christ week after week.

Of all the things we can build and improve, none is more important than our foundation. Christ is the Rock upon which we stand. The truth of his Word, the grace of his gospel, and his command to love our neighbors give us something far more enduring than the buildings and roads around us.

All these things have me thinking about the next ten years.

Springville will undoubtedly continue to change. There will be more homes, more families, more opportunities, and new challenges to meet. I'm thankful that we have the opportunity to keep growing and maturing together.

And I'm thankful that we don't have to choose between appreciating the Springville we've known and welcoming the Springville that's coming.

We can do both.

So this Thanksgiving, I'm grateful for the people who made Springville what it was when we arrived, for those who have helped it grow into what it is today, and for those who will carry it forward.

May the Lord be with us in our endeavors over the next ten years.

And may we never forget to give him thanks.
