A Town Is More Than Its Growth

One neighbor's thoughts on life, faith, and the town we call home.

November 23, 2026

I've been thinking about what makes Springville feel like Springville.

It's not an easy thing to put your finger on. We have our familiar streets, local businesses, schools, churches, and places where people gather. But none of those things, by themselves, really makes a town a community.

It's the people. Or perhaps more accurately, it's the relationships between the people.

And that's been on my mind as Springville considers its future.

The city is currently asking residents to help shape a new comprehensive plan. There's a survey asking what we think about growth, development, transportation, housing, and what we'd like our town to look like in the years ahead.

I'm thankful Springville is taking time to think carefully about its future. There's much to be excited about as our town grows. New families, new opportunities, and new places to gather are all part of a promising future.

And we have something wonderful to build upon.

It's found in the neighbors who look out for one another, the familiar faces around town, the churches that serve our community, and the people who quietly give their time to help others. That spirit of neighborliness is one of Springville's greatest treasures.

As we plan new neighborhoods, improve our roads, and create places for people to gather, we have the opportunity to carry that same spirit into every part of our growing town. Each new family brings another opportunity for friendship. Each new neighborhood offers another place for community to flourish.

And the strongest foundation for our community is Jesus Christ. He is the Rock upon which we stand.

Christ teaches us to love our neighbors as ourselves. Scripture reminds us that every person bears the image of God. Those who have called Springville home for generations and those who arrived just last week share that same God-given dignity.

When our lives are rooted in Christ, that love finds expression in ordinary ways. We welcome the new family down the street. We care for an aging neighbor. We take an interest in the people around us. We share the blessings we've received.

These are already beautiful parts of life in Springville. They're worth celebrating, nurturing, and passing along to those who will call this place home in the years ahead.

That's how we can welcome growth with excitement while preserving the heart of the town we love.
