If I Could Put One Thing on Springville’s Wish List

Could restoring Springville’s historic downtown lake enhance the community spaces we already enjoy?

December 7, 2026

Long before Springville became my home, I would pass through town on Decoration Sunday. My mother remembers the lake that once sat in downtown Springville, but it was gone before my time. The more I learn about our town's history, the more I wish I had seen it.

In 1972, following concerns from state health officials about the city's exposed drinking water supply, the lake was filled with dirt and the springs were capped. This allowed the city to continue using the spring water while protecting it from surface contamination.

I'd love to see Springville get its lake back.

Some might argue that we already have lakes at Big Springs Park. We do, and I'm grateful for them. What excites me about restoring the historic lake is what it could do for downtown as a whole. Between our existing park, the farmers market area, local shops and restaurants, we could make much of downtown Springville feel like one large community park. Restoring the lake would enhance what we've already invested in and give people another reason to spend time downtown.

I have no idea whether restoring the lake is financially or practically possible. I believe our spring is still used as a source of drinking water, and protecting that supply must take priority. Springville's 2008 comprehensive plan identified the need to convert to a filtered drinking water system before reopening the spring. There are engineering challenges that I am certainly not qualified to assess. So I'm just dreaming a little here, but if restoring the lake is ever feasible, I think the idea is worth considering.

One Alabama city worth looking at is Tuscumbia. Its downtown spring is the centerpiece of a beautiful public park and remains a source of drinking water for the city. By treating the water at a separate filtration facility, Tuscumbia has been able to preserve its historic spring for the enjoyment of its residents. Tuscumbia has only about 3,700 more residents than Springville, and given how rapidly our community is growing, I wonder if we could eventually do something similar.

Springville also has existing agreements to purchase water from other communities. Could those arrangements eventually meet our needs without drawing water from the spring? Could advances in water treatment make restoration more practical than it was decades ago?

I would love to know the answers to those questions.

As Springville grows and our city leadership considers what our community might look like twenty or thirty years from now, I hope restoring the lake will find its way onto our wish list. A feasibility study, if one hasn't already been done, might reveal opportunities we haven't considered, and outside funding could help make the project possible.

I'd love for the next generation of Springville residents to grow up enjoying the lake that previous generations remember.
