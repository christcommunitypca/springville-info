## Ownership, mission, and tip selection

- Around Springville is an independent community information project owned and maintained by a Springville resident. Its purpose is to help neighbors find out what is happening in and around Springville. Do not describe it as owned, hosted, sponsored, approved, operated or supervised by Christ Community PCA or its Session. The domain's registration fee being paid by the church does not mean the church has approved it as a ministry. Never alter the church website.
- Treat tip emails as suggestions, not guaranteed listings. Review every legitimate tip for local relevance, timeliness, newsworthiness, practical usefulness, credibility, family-friendly content, safety, and true public benefit. Check original sources, verify all details, and compare against already-published material and other candidate items. Do not let frequent submitters or businesses overwhelm the site.
- Prefer a handful of the most interesting and valuable verified updates to a high volume of routine notices. Generally avoid routine self-promotional business pitches, discounts, repetitive announcements, advertising, and recurring activities with little broader public interest. A substantive new development involving a business may still qualify.
- As a practical anti-flooding default, publish no more than one **routine** item from the same organizer in a rolling 14-day period unless genuinely exceptional new developments justify an exception. Prioritize value rather than message count, pressure, money, relationships, or church affiliation. Do not charge a submission or listing fee or accept pay-to-play publication without explicit owner authorization. It is acceptable to publish zero submitted tips on a given day.
- If selection or verification is uncertain or tips become too numerous to evaluate responsibly, hold for owner review. In the daily report give counts reviewed/selected/held or rejected without disclosing private contact information. Preserve the daily Gmail checks, existing safety rules, deduplication and editorial safeguards in the scheduled automation.

## Positive community-news policy

- **Positive, constructive content only.** Around Springville is a useful and encouraging guide to what is happening in and around Springville, not a police blotter or general bad-news publication. Do not publish stories about arrests, jailing, charges, prosecutions, sentencing, crimes, accidents, injuries, deaths, fires, tragedies, scandals, or other negative incidents. Avoid sensational or fear-driven reporting even when the underlying facts are verified.
- Prioritize verified local achievements, uplifting or interesting history, civic improvements, helpful resources, new openings, worthwhile opportunities, celebrations, family-friendly events and constructive neighborhood updates. The daily *Did you know?* item should also favor engaging or positive local history over grim incidents.
- Do not invent cheerful facts or omit material context from anything that *is* published. Brief, neutral, non-graphic practical notices (e.g. official road closures, urgent schedule changes or severe-weather warnings) may be considered when residents need that information to make immediate decisions; they must not turn into stories about the negative incident behind the alert.
- If suitability under the positive-only policy is uncertain, skip the candidate or hold it for the owner. Do not remove or rewrite existing stories without a separate owner request. This policy overrides any broader eligible-news or regional-incident language elsewhere in instructions.

Reusing the Encyclopedia of Alabama is fine for genuinely strong, different discoveries, but not as an automatic daily default. Keep each article strictly about Springville, Alabama, engaging and positive, with a direct source link and genuinely original writing.
