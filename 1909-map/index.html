Mount Zion Baptist Church

Mountain Street · congregation established after the Civil War

Community contribution
The congregation continues to worship at the historic church site, now addressed as 294 Mountain Drive.
Church building
The earlier wooden church had two bell towers and two front doors. Its construction date is unknown; church property was documented in 1896.
Original structure
Replaced around 1976 on the same property.
Church leaders
L. C. Thornton and R. Pruet represented the congregation in 1883. In 1888, R. H. Vogal was pastor and Emma Galbreth was clerk. C. C. Curry was pastor in 1896 and 1897; F. Thaxton and L. Byers served as clerks in those respective years.

Springville through the years

Springville Baptist Church

Organized 1817 · new sanctuary opened 1871

Founding pastor
Sion Blythe
History
Originally organized as Mount Zion Baptist Church on March 22, 1817. A new sanctuary opened in 1871. By 1909 the congregation was known as Springville Baptist Church; it became First Baptist Church in 1974.
Site status
The present First Baptist Church campus is at 980 Robinson Street, northeast of the cemetery and north of the Presbyterian church. Its oldest existing building dates to 1967. The map marker is a schematic reference to the present campus, not a confirmed location for the 1871 sanctuary used in 1909. The 1967 church history by Mrs. Troy E. Taylor may resolve the question.

Springville through the years

Springville Cemetery

Robinson Street · 1853 / 1861

History
The earliest recorded gravestone is dated 1853. Lewis Herring and James Jones deeded the land to the citizens of Springville in 1861. The historic district nomination also documents graves of enslaved people, probably from the 1850s.
Location
The existing cemetery is on Robinson Street, northwest of the Presbyterian church and southwest of First Baptist. The modern mapped center is approximately 33.77985, −86.47214. The 1997 historic district sketch labels a road Cemetery Street; its exact 1909 name and alignment are unverified. The illustrated boundary and roads are schematic, not a surveyed 1909 parcel.
